Soil & Microbes

The estate is moving toward a lower-synthetic-input approach, with biological inputs and on-farm microbial multiplication being documented as practical experiments.

On-Farm Bioslurry Multiplication

A 225-L mother culture is multiplied through multiple 225-L barrels. A portion is applied through irrigation while a portion is retained as inoculum for the next cycle.

Mother culture→10 × 225 L barrels→Multiplication→85–90% fertigation+10–15% retained

Input philosophy: free from synthetic pesticides, herbicides and fungicides. Reduced synthetic NPK is still being used and is intended to be progressively tapered.

Field Research

Field Notes from LinkedIn

An archive of the early public notes that documented the transition from pharmaceutical research to estate farming. These posts are preserved here as part of the story of SAARTHA ESTATE.

1. Back to the Pavilion—Where My Roots Began

Origin story

After 23 years in corporate and pharmaceutical research, I decided to return to agriculture. This transition was described as a return toward nature, creative control, peace, purpose and direct work with the land, with gratitude for the support of my wife.

Website note: This is a personal account of the career transition and the beginning of the SAARTHA ESTATE journey.

2. My First Coffee Harvest!

First CxR harvest

The first harvest of Robusta (CxR) coffee became an important personal milestone. The experience was described with the same excitement as clearing the GATE examination, but expressed through the wonder of seeing a crop grown and harvested from the estate.

Archive: Early visual record of the first CxR harvest.

3. The Journey to Pure Soil: Transparency in Every Bean

Clean-crop and input transition

The post described a commitment to produce Coffee, Black Pepper and Avocados without synthetic insecticides, pesticides and herbicides, while also documenting the continued use of reduced inorganic NPK and a planned transition toward more organic and biological inputs.

Microbial inputs mentioned included Beauveria bassiana, Metarhizium anisopliae, Lecanicillium lecanii, Trichoderma viride, Pseudomonas and microbial NPK consortia.

Website wording: The estate is described as free from synthetic pesticides, herbicides and fungicides; synthetic NPK use is reduced and being progressively tapered.

4. Coffee Fruit Set, Winkler Irrigation & Multi-Crop Development

Irrigation and establishment

Despite insufficient rainfall, coffee fruit set was encouraging. A Winkler small-sprinkler irrigation system was installed to improve water use, alongside investment in Areca, Pepper, Chandragiri Arabica and CxR Robusta.

The post framed the next 3–4 years as an establishment phase and described a goal of developing a strong diversified multi-crop estate by 2030.

5. Pepper Catkins After the July Rains

Field observation

After insufficient rain in May and June, July showers were followed by renewed pepper growth and fresh catkin emergence. The post also introduced ongoing field research on wilt-affected vines.

A hypothesis was recorded that repeated Bordeaux applications might affect beneficial microbial populations and contribute to pathogen-management challenges. This remains a field hypothesis rather than a confirmed conclusion.

6. Reviving Phytophthora-Affected Pepper Vines

Preliminary case observation

Several old pepper vines affected before the farm was purchased were followed for 5–6 months. The post reported visible regrowth and an estimated 50–60% recovery after 2–3 soil drenches and 2 foliar sprays using a microbial consortium.

The record emphasizes drainage, microbial-rich compost and reduced dependence on synthetic chemical inputs. The observation is retained as a preliminary on-farm case study, not as proof that the microbial treatment alone caused the recovery.

7. Pepper & Chandragiri Harvest

Recent field update

Fresh pepper catkins and vigorous green flush were recorded, along with an early Chandragiri harvest associated in the post with the previous year's unseasonal rains.

The post documented harvesting without synthetic pesticides, fungicides or herbicides and linked the farming journey to more than 20 years of medicinal-chemistry and pharmaceutical research.

Field record: Images associated with this post were dated 11 September 2026 and showed pepper vines and Chandragiri berries at different stages.
